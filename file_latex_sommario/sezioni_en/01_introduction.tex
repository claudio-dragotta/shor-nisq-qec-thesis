\section{Introduction and objectives}

Shor's algorithm factors integers in polynomial time with respect to the binary length of
the input, with implications for the security of cryptographic systems based on
factorization \cite{en-shor1994,en-nist2024pqc,en-gidney2021}. Errors in operations,
decoherence, and imperfect readout, however, can compromise the information needed to find
the multiplicative order \cite{en-preskill2018}.

The thesis studies its sensitivity to noise and evaluates three strategies: outcome
selection through post-processing, isolating the contribution of machine learning; quantum
error correction (QEC) with surface codes and qLDPC codes; gate reduction through the
approximate quantum Fourier transform (AQFT). Each strategy is compared with explicit
references and with metrics consistent with the task. The simulation campaigns are
separate: QEC is evaluated on memory circuits, not within the full Shor algorithm.
