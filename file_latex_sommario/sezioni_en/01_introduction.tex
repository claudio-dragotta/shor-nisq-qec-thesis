\section{Introduction and objectives}

Shor's algorithm factors integers in time polynomial in the input bit length, with
implications for cryptographic systems whose security relies on the difficulty of
factorization \cite{en-shor1994}. Realizing this advantage requires error control
compatible with circuit depth: gate errors, decoherence, and imperfect readout can alter
the measurement distribution and compromise multiplicative-order finding
\cite{en-preskill2018}.

This simulation study determines which interventions improve computational reliability
and relative to which baselines. Its two main phases evaluate classical post-processing
of Shor's outcomes, including an ablation of the machine-learning contribution, and
characterize quantum error-correction codes and syndrome decoders. A separate analysis
examines Shor's sensitivity to gate errors and circuit optimization through the
approximate quantum Fourier transform (AQFT).

The three areas---outcome selection, information protection, and circuit cost
reduction---share one criterion: compare each solution with an explicit baseline,
isolate each component's contribution, and use task-relevant benefit metrics. The
campaigns remain separate; they are not an integrated, error-corrected execution of
Shor's algorithm.
