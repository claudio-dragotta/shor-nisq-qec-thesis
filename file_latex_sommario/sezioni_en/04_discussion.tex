\section{Discussion and conclusions}

The strategies act at distinct levels. Post-processing exploits the residual information
without modifying the quantum state: in the ablation, good classifier metrics do not imply
a lower factorization cost. At high noise intensities, success close to the uniform
reference does not show that quantum information is preserved.

QEC protects information; in memory experiments, learned decoding can help when syndromes
contain correlations not represented by the reference decoder, such as crosstalk. The
qLDPC comparison shows that code structure and decoder jointly set the balance between
protection and data qubits, without measuring gains for Shor's algorithm.

The AQFT reduces opportunities for error. For \(N = 15\), the order \(r = 4\) generates
phases exactly representable with two bits; for \(N = 21\), \(r = 6\) requires phases not
exactly representable and more complex arithmetic. The benefit is not automatic; the
approximation must be chosen per instance and noise, and verified on independent data.

The conclusions remain simulation-based: post-processing on \(N = 15\), QEC on separate
memories, the Gross code in code-capacity, and the AQFT on few instances with one offline
calibration. No fault-tolerant execution of Shor's algorithm is realized, and no resources
are estimated for the cryptographic scale.

Future work should therefore prioritize the controlled reduction of circuit cost,
preserving the information needed for order finding. Besides the AQFT
\cite{en-barencoAQFT1996}, optimizations of modular arithmetic and operation synthesis
should be studied \cite{en-gidney2021}. Quality must be verified through phase-estimation
accuracy and factorization success, not gate count alone. No change in Shor's asymptotic
complexity is shown here.

This direction should accompany future fault-tolerant execution, with logical qubits and
protected operations limiting error propagation. Optimization and QEC are complementary:
the former contains resources, the latter protects execution from noise.
