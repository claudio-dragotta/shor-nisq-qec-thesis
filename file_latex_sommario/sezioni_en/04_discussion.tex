\section{Discussion and conclusions}

Post-processing makes better use of the information contained in the measurements, but
does not act on the quantum state. The ablation study shows that good classifier
performance does not necessarily imply a lower factorization cost, whereas in decoding
the learned model can be useful when syndromes contain correlations that the reference
decoder does not adequately represent.

For \(N = 15\), the AQFT yields a reduction in the number of gates accompanied by an
increase in success probability. Since the order \(r = 4\) generates phases exactly
representable with two control-register bits, the removed rotations contribute little to
the useful information. For \(N = 21\), with \(r = 6\), the phases are instead not
exactly representable and the arithmetic is more complex; the benefit of the
approximation therefore does not transfer automatically. The truncation degree must be
chosen according to the instance and the noise regime, and verified on independent data.

The conclusions remain limited to the simulation context analyzed. Post-processing is
evaluated mainly for \(N = 15\), quantum error correction on separate memory circuits,
and the AQFT on a limited number of instances with a single offline calibration. Neither
a complete fault-tolerant execution of Shor nor an estimate of the resources required for
cryptographic-scale factorization is carried out.

Overall, the work provides a quantitative characterization of the conditions under which
post-processing, decoding, and circuit approximation can be useful. Future developments
may include validation on real hardware, further values of \(N\) and of the order \(r\),
and the integration of protected logical operations, decoding, and circuit optimization.
