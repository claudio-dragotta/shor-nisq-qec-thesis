\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Shor's algorithm, validation, and definition of success}\label{sec:implementation-en}

Given a base \(a\) coprime to the number \(N\) to be factored, the order \(r\), i.e.\ the
period of \(a^{x} \bmod N\), is the smallest positive integer such that
\(a^{r} \equiv 1 \pmod{N}\). The quantum part estimates it through phase estimation and the
inverse quantum Fourier transform (inverse QFT); the classical part derives and verifies
the factors through rational approximation and the greatest common divisor.

The main instance, \(N = 15\) and \(a = 7\), uses eight control qubits and four work
qubits. With \(r = 4\), the ideal outcomes are 0, 64, 128, and 192. Each measured execution
(\emph{shot}) yields one outcome; many shots form a histogram. Success is the recovery of
non-trivial factors, not of the exact order: three ideal peaks lead to it, with a
probability of 75\%. Post-processing, however, accepts 63 of the 256 outcomes, so a uniform
distribution would still give 24.61\% success. This reference depends on the instance and
on the classical verification, and does not measure the fidelity of the quantum state
\cite{en-smolin2013}.

Validation separately checks modular operators, ancilla restoration, and theoretical
distributions. \(N = 21\), \(a = 2\) and \(N = 35\), \(a = 6\) are also validated ideally,
with Beauregard's reversible arithmetic \cite{en-beauregard2002}; only \(N = 21\) enters the
exploratory noisy AQFT tests, owing to limited simulation resources. Qiskit/Aer,
scikit-learn, Stim, PyMatching, and ldpc are used, recording versions, parameters, seeds,
and circuit identifiers.

\subsection{Sensitivity to gate errors}

The main circuit is compiled into RZ, SX, X, and CX gates: depth 412, with 224 CX and 70
SX. After each gate, excluding the virtual RZ gates, independent Pauli errors are
introduced, varying the non-identity error probability \(p_g\) over 13 values between 0 and
0.5. The model is phenomenological and uniform, not calibrated on a real device. The
per-gate probability \(p_g\) is not directly comparable with the logical failure \(p_L\) of
QEC codes: Shor's algorithm is not compiled in fault-tolerant form.

\subsection{Post-processing and machine-learning ablation}

Three criteria select the outcomes to verify on the same 1,024-shot histograms, with a
common factor extractor and tie handling. Each histogram constitutes one iteration,
repeated in case of failure. TOP-1 verifies the most frequent candidate; TOP-4 verifies up
to four, ranked by frequency; Method~2 applies TOP-4 only if a classifier predicts success
for the dominant candidate. The comparison with unfiltered TOP-4 constitutes the ablation:
it separates the contribution of the learned model from the benefit of trying more
candidates.

The illustrative scenarios UC1, the reference, and UC2, the stress case, include
depolarization, thermal relaxation, and readout errors. For each scenario, 2,000
histograms, with parameters varied within \(\pm50\%\) of nominal, are split 60\%/20\%/20\%
into training, selection, and test. Random forests, support vector machines (SVMs), and
multilayer perceptrons are compared on the F1 score; the chosen model is evaluated on 400
independent histograms. The primary metric is the number of iterations needed to obtain the
factors, with 30 paired replicates and at most 50 iterations; accuracy is secondary and run
times depend on the available resources.

\subsection{Quantum error correction: surface code and qLDPC}

QEC distributes the information of one logical qubit over several physical qubits.
Syndromes flag errors without directly measuring the logical state and guide the decoder in
the correction; the metric is the logical failure probability \(p_L\). After preliminary
checks with the repetition code and the Steane code, surface-code memories
\cite{en-steane1996,en-fowler2012,en-googleQECBelowThreshold2025} are studied at increasing
distance \(d\), a parameter linked to the correction capability, with operation,
preparation, and measurement errors and, in some configurations, crosstalk between adjacent
qubits.

The reference decoder, \emph{minimum-weight perfect matching} (MWPM), pairs the triggered
syndromes. It is compared with a stand-alone neural network and with a hybrid decoder, in
which a network decides when to override the MWPM choice. The intervention threshold is
selected on the validation set and fixed on the test set \cite{en-alphaqubit2024}. Since
correction circuits contain only Clifford gates and measurements, they can be simulated
efficiently on a classical computer: Stim samples their syndromes over millions of runs,
and the decoders, including the neural network, are trained and evaluated on these data.

An exploratory comparison considers IBM's Gross code, a quantum low-density parity-check
(qLDPC) code with 12 logical qubits and 144 data qubits \cite{en-bravyi2024}. The
\emph{code-capacity} model is used: errors on data qubits only and perfect syndromes.
BP+OSD (\emph{belief propagation with ordered statistics decoding}) is used on the Gross
code and as a further reference on the surface code \cite{en-roffe2020}. For the Gross
code, several configurations are compared, including parallel and serial message updates.

\subsection{Reducing circuit cost with the AQFT}

The AQFT removes small-angle controlled rotations, reducing gates and depth at the expense
of phase precision \cite{en-barencoAQFT1996}. The question is whether the noise avoided
compensates for this loss. It is applied to Shor's final inverse QFT for \(N = 15\) and to
the QFTs inside the modular arithmetic for \(N = 21\); separate phase-estimation
benchmarks, on registers of six to ten qubits, isolate its effect.

The noise is derived from the gate infidelities and readout errors of the offline
FakeSherbrooke calibration, without additional \(T_1/T_2\) contributions; layouts and
random seeds are kept unchanged. The approximation degree, i.e.\ how many rotations to
remove, is chosen on selection data and evaluated on independent data. The 8,192 shots per
configuration of the \(N = 15\) pilot and the 256 for \(N = 21\) are split equally between
selection and test.
