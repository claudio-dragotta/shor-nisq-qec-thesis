\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation and contribution of post-processing}

Ideal arithmetic checks confirm the correctness of the circuits in the absence of noise
for the main instance \(N = 15\) and for the instances \(N = 21\) and \(N = 35\), with a
total variation distance from the theoretical distribution of \(1.38 \times 10^{-9}\) for
\(N = 21\) and \(2.50 \times 10^{-13}\) for \(N = 35\).

In scenarios UC1 and UC2, the selected SVMs reach F1 scores of 0.919 and 0.923 and areas
under the ROC curve of 0.965 and 0.958. This predictive ability, however, does not reduce
the number of iterations required. TOP-4 needs 1.00 iterations on average in both
scenarios, versus 1.37 and 1.50 for TOP-1, with statistically significant differences in
paired tests; Method 2 instead needs 1.50 and 1.37 iterations, without significantly
improving on TOP-1 and performing worse than TOP-4. The observed advantage therefore
appears to stem mainly from the possibility of considering more candidates. These results
nonetheless remain tied to the instance, the noise, and the methods considered.

\subsection{Memory protection and decoding}

The initial checks on the repetition and Steane codes show the expected behavior. Within
Monte Carlo uncertainty, the repetition code reproduces the relation
\(p_{L} = 3p^{2} - 2p^{3}\); for Steane, the dependence on the physical error is
approximately quadratic, with a logarithmic slope of 1.94 and a pseudo-threshold around
0.08.

The main analysis concerns the surface code. Below threshold, increasing the distance
reduces the logical error, while above threshold the opposite behavior is observed; the
fit gives values of about 0.86\% in the \(Z\) basis and 0.81\% in the \(X\) basis.

Under nominal noise, the stand-alone neural network shows no significant advantage over
MWPM. With crosstalk, however, the hybrid decoder improves performance: at \(d = 3\),
\(p = 0.003\), and crosstalk 0.01, \(p_{L}\) falls from 0.05829 to 0.04851, a relative
reduction of about 16.8\%, significant by paired McNemar test. The benefit shrinks at
distance 5 and is not significant at distance 7.

BP+OSD is considered as a further comparison and, in the distance-5 configurations
analyzed, outperforms the MWPM-based hybrid. The tests also show that performance depends
on the distance, the number of rounds, and the size of the network input. The Gross code
protects 12 logical qubits with 144 data qubits better than 12 distance-13 surface-code
patches (2,028 data qubits) for \(p \geq 0.75\%\) and than distance-11 patches at every
noise level; with serial scheduling of belief propagation the logical error at
\(p = 1\%\) drops by about a hundred times.

\subsection{Factorization success and the uniform reference}

Without noise, single-shot success is 74.89\% (95\% confidence interval
{[}74.59\%; 75.18\%{]}), consistent with the expected 75\%. As \(p_{g}\) increases,
success decreases down to 24.59\% at \(p_{g} = 0.5\), very close to the 24.61\% uniform
reference (Section~\ref{sec:implementation-en}). This shows that the number of correct
factorizations alone is not sufficient to describe how much quantum structure remains in
the circuit.

\subsection{Effectiveness and limitations of the approximate QFT}

In the Shor pilot for \(N = 15\), the selected AQFT raises the single-shot success
probability from 47.92\% to 62.79\%, an increase of 14.87 percentage points (+31\% relative)
on independent test data (95\% Newcombe confidence interval
{[}12.73; 16.99{]}). At the same time, ECR gates decrease from 362 to 279 (\(-22.9\%\))
and circuit depth from 1,351 to 1,141 (\(-15.5\%\)). The
improvement therefore concerns factorization directly, not only phase estimation.

For \(N = 21\), truncating the QFTs inside the arithmetic reduces ECR gates from 49,661
to 23,178, roughly by half (\(-53.3\%\)), but ideal success drops from 46.67\% to
40.13\% (\(-6.54\) percentage points). In the three noisy
comparisons, with only 128 test shots per variant, the intervals of the differences always
include zero. In the nine phase-estimation benchmarks, from six to ten qubits, all
differences favor the selected variant, up to 12.55 percentage points ({[}9.77; 15.30{]}). The comparisons remain exploratory, and the best
truncation degree among those tested, two or three, cannot be considered valid in general
for registers of arbitrary size.
