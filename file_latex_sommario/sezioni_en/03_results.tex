\section{Results}

\subsection{Validation and sensitivity of Shor's algorithm to noise}

Ideal checks confirm that the circuits are correct; the total variation distance from the
theoretical distribution is \(1.38 \times 10^{-9}\) for \(N = 21\) and
\(2.50 \times 10^{-13}\) for \(N = 35\). For each \(p_g\), 20 replicates of 4,096 shots are
run. Noise-free success is 74.89\% (95\% confidence interval {[}74.59\%; 75.18\%{]}),
consistent with the expected 75\%. It falls to 72.5\%, 56.5\%, 45.2\%, and 29.5\% for
\(p_g\) equal to 0.1\%, 1\%, 2\%, and 5\%, respectively. For \(p_g \geq 10\%\) it stays
around 25\%, down to 24.59\% at \(p_g = 0.5\), close to the uniform reference of 24.61\%.
With 294 exposed gates, \(p_g = 1\%\) implies about three errors on average per run.

\subsection{Post-processing and machine learning}

The selected SVMs reach, in UC1 and UC2, F1 scores of 0.919 and 0.923 and areas under the
ROC curve of 0.965 and 0.958. This predictive ability, however, does not reduce the
iterations. TOP-4 obtains the factors at the first iteration in the observed replicates of
both scenarios: mean 1.00, versus 1.37 and 1.50 for TOP-1, with significant differences in
paired tests. Method~2 instead requires 1.50 and 1.37 iterations: it does not
significantly improve on TOP-1 and is worse than TOP-4. The benefit thus comes from the
additional candidates, not the classifier, under the conditions studied.

\subsection{Quantum error correction}\label{sec:results-qec-en}

Denoting by \(p\) the physical error probability, the repetition code reproduces, within
Monte Carlo uncertainty, \(p_L = 3p^2 - 2p^3\); the Steane code shows an approximately
quadratic dependence on the physical error, with a logarithmic slope of 1.94 and a
pseudo-threshold of about 0.08. In the surface code, increasing the distance reduces the
logical error below threshold and increases it above threshold. The fit estimates
thresholds of about 0.86\% in the \(Z\) basis and 0.81\% in the \(X\) basis, in this model.

Under nominal noise, the stand-alone network does not significantly outperform MWPM. With
crosstalk, for \(d = 3\), \(p = 0.003\), and crosstalk 0.01, the hybrid decoder reduces
\(p_L\) from 0.05829 to 0.04851: \(-16.8\%\) relative, significant by the paired McNemar
test. The benefit shrinks at \(d = 5\) and is not significant at \(d = 7\); in the
distance-5 configurations BP+OSD outperforms the hybrid. Performance also depends on the
number of correction rounds and on the size of the network input.

In the code-capacity model, the Gross code protects 12 logical qubits with 144 data qubits
better than 12 independent surface-code patches at distance 11 (1,452 data qubits) at all
noise levels studied, and better than distance-13 patches (2,028) for
\(p \geq 0.75\%\). At \(p = 1\%\) the block failure is \(1.0 \times 10^{-6}\), versus
\(1.6 \times 10^{-6}\) for the distance-13 patches. With serial updates, the Gross code
corrects all errors of up to five qubits, verified over about \(5 \times 10^{8}\)
configurations; at \(p = 1\%\) the logical error is about a hundred times lower than with
the initial parallel configuration, which already failed with three errors. The advantage
therefore depends on the decoder, in this model.

\subsection{Effects of the AQFT}

For \(N = 15\), on the test data the selected AQFT raises per-shot success from 47.92\% to
62.79\%: +14.87 percentage points (+31\% relative), with a 95\% Newcombe confidence
interval of {[}12.73; 16.99{]}. Two-qubit ECR gates decrease from 362 to 279
(\(-22.9\%\)) and depth from 1,351 to 1,141 (\(-15.5\%\)). The benefit concerns
factorization directly.

For \(N = 21\), ECR gates drop from 49,661 to 23,178 (\(-53.3\%\)), but ideal success falls
from 46.67\% to 40.13\% (\(-6.54\) percentage points). In the three noisy comparisons, with
128 test shots per variant, the intervals of the differences include zero: a benefit is
not demonstrated. In the nine phase-estimation benchmarks all differences favor the AQFT,
up to 12.55 percentage points ({[}9.77; 15.30{]}). The comparisons remain exploratory: the
best approximation degree among those tested, two or three, cannot be generalized to
arbitrary registers.
